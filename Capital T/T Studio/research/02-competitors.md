## A. The substitute next door: creators selling UGC on Khamsat (checked Nov 21)

**Source:** Khamsat's [human UGC category](https://khamsat.com/video-design/human-ugc). 30 listings are shown; more load only in a browser, so this is a sample, not the whole market. I opened 9 listings in full and pulled reviews from 21.

| What | Finding |
|---|---|
| Starting prices | $5–90; median about $37.50. 5 listings at $5–10, 7 at $15–25, 13 at $35–50, 5 at $80–90 |
| What the starting price buys | Often raw footage or 15–20 s. Editing, a script, a longer video, a female presenter or a Gulf dialect are paid extras (e.g. editing +$25–30, script +$5–15, Saudi dialect +$50–75) |
| Edited video within $35 | 1 of the 9 listings opened included editing in a starting price of $35 or less |
| Dialect | Listings rarely state Egyptian dialect. Two of the 9 present in Saudi or formal Arabic, and Gulf dialect is sold at a premium |
| Delivery | 1 day to 1 week; most 3 days |
| Reviews | 21 of 30 listings have reviews (205 in total); 9 have none |
| AI listings | The wider UGC category also sells "AI UGC" from $5–50 (9 of the first 30 in the main category) |

**What it means (inference):**
- A brand can already buy a filmed video from an Arab creator for $25–90 without a middleman.
- But a video that is ready to run as an ad (Egyptian dialect, edited, captioned, with a hook) usually costs more than the starting price suggests.
- And the brand still has to write the brief and script itself, choose the creator and check the result.

That gap is where a studio could add value. It's a hypothesis until buyers confirm it.

## B. What buyers of UGC value: Khamsat review mining (Nov 21)

**Method:**
- **Data:** 176 non-empty public buyer reviews on 21 human-UGC listings, the up-to-25 most recent per listing; names removed.
- **Coding:** keyword matching in Arabic. One review can count in several themes.
- **Limits:** it's an approximate method. Public reviews are mostly positive, so they show what buyers value, not what went wrong.

| Theme | Reviews | Share |
|---|---|---|
| Professional, cooperative, good manners | 70 | 40% |
| Quality of image, sound, editing | 41 | 23% |
| Speed or on-time delivery | 38 | 22% |
| Will buy again / repeat order | 26 | 15% |
| On-camera presence and delivery | 14 | 8% |
| Understood the brief, right first time | 9 | 5% |
| Beat expectations | 9 | 5% |
| Revisions handled well | 8 | 5% |
| Ideas, script, creative input | 5 | 3% |
| Price or value for money | 2 | 1% |

- **Reservations:** only 1 of 176 ("needs to be longer").
- **Dialect of the reviews:** inconclusive. 15 use Gulf words, 10 Egyptian words, and the rest are too short or formal to tell.

**What it means (inference):**
- Buyers judge a UGC supplier first on reliability and how easy they are to work with, then on quality and speed.
- Repeat orders are common, so the need recurs.
- Scripts and ideas are rarely praised. Either buyers don't expect them from a creator, or they don't value them. Interview question 5 tests this.

Verbatim quotes are in [00-context.md §9](00-context.md#9-voice-of-the-customer-verbatim).

**Read so far:** T Studio wouldn't be the first to offer "managed Arabic UGC".
- **Platforms** (Stllr, UGC MENA, Arabic Creators) offer scale and self-service, mostly Saudi-centred.
- **A local agency** (influUGC) offers a full service in Egypt and Saudi Arabia.
- **Hundreds of freelancers** on Khamsat, Fiverr and Collabstr sell single videos for $25–300.

So "we do UGC" isn't a difference. The angle must be narrower; see §E.
